# Atomic Notes

A note stays atomic when it covers one idea well enough to stand alone, and links out to related
ideas rather than absorbing them. The alternative — one long note per broad topic — makes
wikilinks nearly useless: a link into a 2,000-word omnibus note points at "somewhere in here," not
at a specific claim.

## Why this vault enforces it structurally

Most notes here run 15–40 lines on purpose. That's not a stylistic preference — it's what keeps
the wikilink graph meaningful: a link target should be small enough that arriving at it answers
the question the link implied. [[Species-Accounts-Workflow]] versus a single giant "everything
about birds" note is the same trade-off in the birding cluster: five short, linked notes instead
of one long one.

## The failure mode on the other side

Splitting too aggressively produces notes so thin they're not worth the link overhead — a single
sentence with three lines of frontmatter around it. Atomicity is about matching note size to idea
size, not about minimizing size for its own sake.
